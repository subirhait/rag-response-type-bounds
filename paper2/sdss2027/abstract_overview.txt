Stochastic LLM evaluation usually fixes a replication count and spends the same number of generations on every query. We introduce disagreement-triggered replication: generate twice in each regime, stop when the two outcomes agree, and continue to a maximum K only after disagreement. We prove that the K+1 stopped-history likelihoods form a basis for the same degree-K polynomial space identified by K uniform Bernoulli replications. Thus the adaptive design preserves the finite-K population identification needed for certified component-response prevalence while reducing expected generation cost. Its expected cost is 2+2(K-2)p(1-p) per query-regime pair, implying at least a (K-2)/(2K) expected saving for every latent distribution. Retrospectively applying the rule to ordered HotpotQA outputs stops 97.9% of 3,000 query-regime cells after two draws and uses 6,189 rather than 15,000 generations, a 58.7% saving. At nearly the same realized cost, however, fixed K=2 gives a shorter exact projection interval, showing that identification preservation does not imply finite-sample efficiency. In a corner-concentrated simulation, reinvesting adaptive savings in more queries reduces mean certified width by about 14% relative to uniform K=5; at larger budgets the adaptive design also overtakes K=2. The work turns replication count into an explicit identification-versus-estimation design problem.
